Week 2 homework · the pull-up

Your Practice

This week was all about the pull. The single most important thing you can do at home is train the pull, because a pull-up is built by pulling, and one class a week is not enough on its own. Keep it short and do it often — consistency beats intensity at this stage.

Week 1 Week 2

Pick your level

Pick the level that matches where you are, block by block. A few of these could sit a level higher or lower depending on the person — if in doubt, take the easier one and do it properly.

The golden rule from class

Explode up on the first reps, fight the lowering slow on the last ones.

Getting the best out of it

Two sessions of fifteen to twenty minutes beats one long one. Even one honest session this week is a real week of training.

Two short sessions

Fifteen to twenty minutes each. Any two days with a day between them.

Warm up for three minutes first: wrists, shoulders, hips, twenty squats, one hang.

Each session

One pull · one push · one hold. Three things, that is it.

The pull comes first, while you are fresh. Do it in both sessions.

Every day

Hang from something. A bar, a door frame, a beam — ten to thirty seconds, once or twice, whenever you walk past.

It costs nothing, and this one habit is worth more than the rest put together.
