Your Handstand

The handstand is one line — wrist to shoulder to ribs to hips — stood on its head. This week builds the bottom of that line and teaches you how to get out of a handstand before you spend any time trying to stay in one.

Week 1 Week 2

Pick your level

Same numbers as your calisthenics card. If you have never held a chest-to-wall handstand, start at 4 — there is no prize for starting higher.

Getting the best out of it

Balance is a skill, not a muscle. Ten focused minutes on three days beats one long session where the wrists give out in the first quarter.

Three short sessions

Ten to fifteen minutes each. Balance work wants frequency, not duration.

Always in this order: wrists, line, push, entry, balance. Never start upside down cold.

Each session

Wrists · one line drill · one push · kick-ups · one balance hold.

Practise the bail twice at the start of every session, even once you stop needing it.

Stop early

When the shape breaks or the wrists complain, the set is over. Upside down is not the place to grind.

Breathe the whole time. Holding your breath is the most common fault in the room.
